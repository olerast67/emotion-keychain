## 1. Design principles for character faces on a tiny 1-bit screen (readability, strokes, eye sizes, eyes-only, ring vs filled, dithering, glow)

### Takeaway
On a 0.96" 128x64 panel each pixel is 0.17 mm, so the whole face is only ~22x11 mm. Every serious open-source/commercial robot face on 128x64 uses **big, bold, high-contrast blobs**: eyes 28–40 px tall (≈45–65 % of screen height), 8–10 px corner radius, and emotion carried mainly by **eyelid cuts (angle, height, bend)** and eye proportions rather than by fine detail. Anki removed Cozmo's pupils on purpose; emotion lives in shape, size and ratio.

### Cited Findings
**Physical size of the canvas**
- Typical 0.96" 128x64 SSD1306 panel: active (display) area 21.74 x 10.86 mm, pixel pitch 0.17 x 0.17 mm, pixel size 0.154 x 0.154 mm, panel 26.70 x 19.26 mm — [SunFounder OLED spec](https://docs.sunfounder.com/projects/pironman5/en/latest/hardware/oled_screen.html); same 21.74 x 10.86 mm active area and 0.17 mm pitch (pixel size 0.148 mm) in other vendors' sheets — [Easby REA012864D](https://easby.com/?p=2762), [Texim Europe listing](https://catalogue.texim-europe.com/product/Display-and-Touch-Solutions/Display-Panels/OLED-Display/PMOLED/detail/WEA012864DWPP3N00002-WSR). (Pixel size differs between variants: 0.154 vs 0.148 mm.)
- Yellow/blue "bicolor" 0.96" panels: the top 16 rows are yellow and the remaining 48 rows are blue (fixed color stripes, still a 1-bit SSD1306 panel) — [Makers Electronics](https://makerselectronics.com/product/oled-display-i2c-4-pin-0-96%e2%80%b3/), [52Pi](https://www.52pi.com/products/52pi-oled-display-module-i2c-iic-128x64-pixel-0-96-inch-display-module-yellow-blue-two-color-display-compatible-with-raspberry-pi-arduino-51-series-mcu-stm32-r3-and-mega). One of the vendor listings in the same search phrased it as "typically 16 pixels" and another warned "actual product may vary", so verify on the purchased unit — (search summary over the listings above and [Embedded Adventures OLED-12864-YB](https://embeddedadventures.com/oled_display_128x64_oled-12864-yb.html); exact attribution of the quote not confirmed).

**Eye sizes that real projects use on 128x64**
- Anki Cozmo (V1, 128x64 face display): nominal eye **30 px wide x 40 px tall**, nominal eye spacing 64 px (Vector V2 at 184x96 scales this to 43x57, spacing 92) — Vector source [proceduralFace.h](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFace.h) and [proceduralFace.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFace.cpp) (comments "V1 width: 128 / height: 64", "NominalEyeHeight V2: 57; V1: 40", "NominalEyeWidth V2: 43; V1: 30", "kProcFace_NominalEyeSpacing … V1: 64").
- pycozmo (Python re-implementation of Cozmo's procedural face): 128x64 canvas, default eye 28x40 px, eyes offset ±25 px from center, rendered in PIL 1-bit mode "1" — [pycozmo procedural_face.py](https://github.com/zayfod/pycozmo/blob/master/pycozmo/procedural_face.py).
- FluxGarage RoboEyes defaults: eye 36x36 px, border radius 8 px, space between eyes 10 px, top-eyelid max height = eye height/2 — [RoboEyes header](https://github.com/FluxGarage/RoboEyes/blob/main/src/FluxGarage_RoboEyes.h).
- esp32-eyes "Normal" preset: 40x40 px, radius 8/8; "Surprised" 45x45 radius 16 — [EyePresets.h](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/EyePresets.h).
- intellar oled_eye_display: REF_EYE 40x40 px, space between eyes 10 px, corner radius 10 px — [control_display.ino](https://github.com/intellar/oled_eye_display/blob/main/control_display/control_display.ino).

**Why "eyes-only" faces read so well (Cozmo/Vector/EMO)**
- Cozmo originally had pupils (helped people see where he looked), but Anki's Hanns Tappeiner said they made him feel too human, "so the animators changed the size and ratio of the eyes" instead — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/cozmo-ai-robot-brings-wall-e-to-life/).
- Anki borrowed the idea of EVE's eyes from WALL-E "right down to the color and aesthetic" — [Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/54328/Whats-the-One-Design-Element-That-Can-Make-a-Robot-Lovable); much of Cozmo's "heavy lifting is left to his eyes", animated by a team from Pixar and DreamWorks — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/06/27/cozmo/); former Pixar animator Carlos Baena led the animation team — [Digital Trends (via search summary)](https://digitaltrends.com/?p=994925), [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2016/06/27/anki-pixar-dreamworks-robot-games).
- In Cozmo-style parametric faces, the emotions are almost entirely eyelid geometry: pycozmo's expression presets (based on Catherine Chambers' "Expressive Eyes" project) define e.g. Anger = upper lid y 0.6 with angle −30°/+30° (left/right), Sadness = upper lid y 0.6 angle +20°/−20°, Happiness = lower lid y 0.4 with bend 0.4 and fully rounded upper corners, Surprise = eye scale 1.25, Neutral = scale 0.8 — [pycozmo expressions.py](https://github.com/zayfod/pycozmo/blob/master/pycozmo/expressions/expressions.py).
- EMO (Living.AI) marketing claims "over 1000 unique expressions and movements" and asymmetric expressions such as "a cheeky wink or a pout" — [Keyi Robot blog (retailer, marketing-level)](https://keyirobot.com/en-us/blogs/buying-guide/inside-emo-robots-expressive-design-and-how-it-connects-with-you).

**Kaomoji/emoticon-style faces**
- pwnagotchi uses pure text kaomoji faces on its e-ink screen, a ready-made "emotion vocabulary": LOOK_R `( ⚆_⚆)`, LOOK_L `(☉_☉ )`, SLEEP `(⇀‿‿↼)`, AWAKE `(◕‿‿◕)`, BORED `(-__-)`, INTENSE `(°▃▃°)`, COOL `(⌐■_■)`, HAPPY `(•‿‿•)`, GRATEFUL `(^‿‿^)`, EXCITED `(ᵔ◡◡ᵔ)`, MOTIVATED `(☼‿‿☼)`, DEMOTIVATED `(≖__≖)`, SMART `(✜‿‿✜)`, LONELY `(ب__ب)`, SAD `(╥☁╥ )`, ANGRY `(-_-')`, FRIEND `(♥‿‿♥)`, BROKEN `(☓‿‿☓)`, DEBUG `(#__#)`, UPLOAD `(1__0)` — [pwnagotchi faces.py](https://github.com/evilsocket/pwnagotchi/blob/master/pwnagotchi/ui/faces.py).

**Dithering on 1-bit**
- Dithering places pixels of two colors in repeating or semi-random patterns; alternating black/white reads as gray from a distance — [pixnote dithering guide](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/dithering); the checkerboard is the simplest pattern but "can look mechanical at high zoom levels"; ordered (Bayer) dithering uses a matrix — [Divoom guide](https://divoom.com/blogs/setup-ideas/pixel-art-dithering-when-to-use-and-stop).
- In 1-bit art "the main challenge is making shapes read clearly with only two colors" (developer comment) and hand-painting areas of a dithered image improved readability vs. a processed hi-res image — [itch.io devlog "Figuring dithering"](https://mik2121.itch.io/wip-playdate-title/devlog/702301/figuring-dithering) (via search summary).
- Lospec is described as the biggest collection of pixel-art tutorials, with downloadable palettes (incl. 1-bit) — [Boing Boing on Lospec](https://boingboing.net/2019/10/01/lospec-a-resource-site-for.html); 1-bit drawing examples — [artnoob substack](https://artnoob.substack.com/p/1bit-pixel-art-drawings).
- Anki rendered Vector's (color) face with anti-aliasing (box filter, size 3), a "glow"/hot-spot render, optional scanlines (disabled by default), and per-frame lightness noise — [proceduralFaceDrawer.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFaceDrawer.cpp). None of this exists on a 1-bit panel, so glow/AA must be faked (see inferences).
- pycozmo notes that the Cozmo protocol expects a 128x32 image, so it "take[s] only the even lines" of a 128x64 render — [pycozmo procedural_face_expressions.py](https://github.com/zayfod/pycozmo/blob/master/examples/procedural_face_expressions.py) — i.e. Cozmo's iconic look is line-doubled/scanlined at 1-bit.

### Inferences
- **Physical math (inference):** 1 px = 0.17 mm; a 30-px eye = 5.1 mm; a 3-px stroke = 0.51 mm. At a 30 cm viewing distance one pixel subtends ≈2 arcmin (0.17/300 rad); at 50 cm ≈1.2 arcmin. With normal acuity ≈1 arcmin (standard figure, not fetched here), 1-px lines are just resolvable and look thin/weak, especially on the 0.148–0.154 mm emitters with dark gaps. **Rule: structural strokes ≥2 px (rings, mouths, brows 2–3 px), important negative space ≥2 px, no feature smaller than 3x3 px except deliberate "sparkle" accents.**
- **Recommended face grid for 128x64 (proposal):** margins 2–4 px; eyebrow band y≈4–10; eyes centered at y≈28–30, x≈38–40 and 88–90 (center-to-center 48–52 px; Cozmo used 64 px with 30-px eyes, RoboEyes/intellar use 10 px gaps with 36–40 px eyes); eye boxes 30–36 px; cheeks/blush at y≈40–46 outside the eyes; mouth band y≈48–58 centered at x=64, 8–16 px wide, 2–3 px thick. A face with a mouth (like the reference nekoto-style face) needs smaller eyes (~28–32 px) than eyes-only faces (36–40 px) to leave vertical room.
- **Bicolor panel (proposal):** keep the eyes entirely in the blue 48 rows (y≥17) and put "overlay" elements in the yellow 16-row band: eyebrows, Zzz, hearts, "!"/"?" marks, music notes, battery/charging icons, notification text. Never let an eye straddle y=15/16 — the color break cuts the shape. Provide a layout flag (mono vs bicolor) that shifts the face grid down ~6–8 px.
- **Ring eyes vs filled rounded-rect eyes (inference):**
  - Filled rounded rectangles (Cozmo/RoboEyes/esp32-eyes) maximize lit area → read best at arm's length, tween trivially (just width/height/radius and black lid masks), and survive 1-bit aliasing. Best for an "eyes-only" pet.
  - Rings (outline + pupil highlight, as on the reference device) read cuter/more "anime" and leave room for a mouth, but: stroke must be ≥3 px (ideally 3–4 px on a 28–32 px ring) or it shimmers/looks broken when the ring moves by 1 px; eyelid cuts must redraw the lid edge as a 2–3 px line, otherwise a cut ring looks like a broken "C"; the pupil/highlight should be a filled disc (6–10 px) plus a 2–3 px black (or white) specular notch, offset consistently (e.g. up-left) in all frames — consistency of the highlight direction is what sells "same character".
  - Hybrid trick: render eye = filled outer shape minus filled inner shape (ring), apply lids as masks to the combined result, then stroke the lid edge.
- **Dithering verdict (inference):** OLED is emissive and very high contrast, so 50 % checkerboards look like a dim grey at a distance but (a) "crawl"/shimmer when the dithered object moves 1 px per frame and (b) muddy small shapes. Use dither only for **static or screen-anchored** effects: soft "glow" halos (a 1–2 px ring of 25–50 % ordered dither around the eye), shadows/blush fills, fades (Bayer 4x4 threshold sweep = 16-step dissolve), and backgrounds. Anchor the Bayer matrix to screen coordinates, not object coordinates, so moving shapes do not flicker. Never Floyd–Steinberg animated content (error diffusion changes every frame → noise).
- **Simulated glow (inference):** options in increasing cost: (1) a 1-px gap then 1-px dotted outline around the eye; (2) an ordered-dither halo 2 px wide; (3) global brightness "breathing" via the SSD1306 contrast register (hardware researchers should confirm the command and range); (4) temporal dithering (alternate frames) — risky: visible flicker below ~60 Hz full-frame refresh.
- **Character design rules (inference, from the cited numbers):** silhouette first (an emotion must be readable from the eye silhouette alone, i.e. staging); asymmetry is the cheapest "life" (one lid 10–20 % lower, one eye 5–10 % bigger when looking sideways — exactly what Vector's LookAt does, see §4); keep a fixed design language: one corner radius family (e.g. 8–10 px for eyes, 2 px for marks), one stroke weight family (2 px marks, 3 px structural), one highlight direction.

## 2. Animation principles applied to robot eyes (12 principles, blink timing, saccades, idle breathing, Cozmo/Vector practice)

### Takeaway
Anki's shipping Vector code is the best "ground truth": a blink is a 7-keyframe, ~330 ms **squash-and-stretch** sequence (eyes get shorter AND wider, up to 5x width when closed), blinks occur every 3–10 s at random, small "eye darts" (saccades) every 1–2.25 s up to ~15 px with 1–2 squashed in-between frames, all layered on top of whatever expression is playing. Combine that with anticipation/overshoot and ease-in/out and the face feels alive.

### Cited Findings
**The 12 principles (Thomas & Johnston, The Illusion of Life, 1981)**
- List: squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, straight-ahead and pose-to-pose, follow-through and overlapping action, slow in and slow out, arcs, secondary action, timing, exaggeration, solid drawing, appeal — [UAL student summary](https://guanzewu.myblog.arts.ac.uk/2021/10/13/week_02-research-of-12-principles-animation/), [EVL/UIC handout PDF](https://www.evl.uic.edu/datsoupi/251/docs/12AnimationPrinciples.pdf), [WKU page](https://people.wku.edu/joon.sung/edu/anim/3d/animation/principles.html) (secondary sources).
- Squash & stretch: volume should stay constant while deforming (a stretched ball gets thinner); slow in/out: "movements start slowly, build speed and finish slowly"; more drawings = slower action — same secondary sources.
- Robots: Takayama, Dooley (Pixar) & Ju, "Expressing Thought: Improving Robot Readability with Animation Principles" (HRI 2011): **anticipation (forethought) and reaction** to task outcome improved how readable a robot's behavior was (online video study, N=273) — [paper PDF](https://www.wendyju.com/publications/151r-takayama.pdf), [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/1957656.1957674). A later systematic review says the twelve principles remain under-explored in HRI and that staging (readability from silhouette) helps monitoring at a distance — [arXiv 1812.06784](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.06784).

**Anki Vector blink — squash & stretch in shipping code**
- `GetNextBlinkFrame` keyframes (height, width multipliers relative to the current eye, time per key): (0.85, 1.05, 33 ms) → (0.6, 1.2, 33) → (0.1, 2.5, 33) → **closed (0.05, 5.0, 33)** → just-opened (0.15, 2.0, 33) → (0.7, 1.2, 33) → (0.9, 1.0, 100 ms) → restore original (33 ms). At the closed frame both eyes are moved to their average Y "so the blink line when completely closed is nice and horizontal" and **all lid parameters are zeroed** — [proceduralFaceDrawer.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFaceDrawer.cpp). (Total ≈ 6x33 + 100 + 33 ≈ 330 ms; closing ≈100 ms, opening ≈170 ms → asymmetric, faster close/slower open.)
- Blink spacing: uniform random 3000–10000 ms (`kKeepAliveBlink_SpacingMinTime_ms` 3000, `…MaxTime_ms` 10000); a screen-protection cap of 30000 ms — [faceLayerManager.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/animProcess/src/cozmoAnim/animation/trackLayerManagers/faceLayerManager.cpp).
- Docking "squint": eye scaleY 0.35, scaleX 1.05, upper-lid angle −10°, interpolated over 250 ms — same file.

**Anki Vector eye darts (saccades) and gaze**
- Eye-dart spacing random 1000–2250 ms; max distance from center 15 px (1 px when "focused"); when darting up eyes scale up to 1.05, down to 0.9; outer eye +3 %; darts longer than 5 px get one in-between frame with squash 0.85 (shift fraction 0.2); darts longer than 10 px get two in-betweens with squash 0.7 and 0.85 (shift fractions 0.2, 0.4); a "ShiftLagFraction" 0.4 parameter — [faceLayerManager.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/animProcess/src/cozmoAnim/animation/trackLayerManagers/faceLayerManager.cpp). (15 px on Vector's 184-px-wide screen ≈ 10 px on 128 px.)
- `LookAt()` defaults: looking up scales eyes to 1.1, down to 0.85; the "outer" eye (in the look direction) gets up to +10 % height while the inner eye shrinks by the same; looking down pushes the eyes together by up to 2 px (interocular distance) — [proceduralFace.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFace.cpp), [proceduralFace.h](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFace.h).
- Keep-alive is implemented as separate concurrent **layers**: "KeepAliveEyeBlink", "KeepAliveEyeDart", "KeepAliveEyeNoise", added on top of canned animations — [trackLayerComponent.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/animProcess/src/cozmoAnim/animation/trackLayerComponent.cpp). Eye "noise" cycles 5 frames of lightness between 0.92 and 1.14 (color display) — [proceduralFaceDrawer.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFaceDrawer.cpp).
- Interpolation between faces is linear per parameter, angles are blended as angles, and an option makes pupils "jump when fraction crosses 0.5" (saccade-like) instead of sliding — [proceduralFace.h](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFace.h).

**Other open-source timings (for comparison)**
- esp32-eyes blink: trapezium 40 ms closing / 100 ms closed / 40 ms opening, value squared (t²) for easing; during the blink corner radii and slopes are driven to 0 — [EyeBlink.cpp](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/EyeBlink.cpp), [Animations.h](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/Animations.h); random blink timer 3500 ms — [BlinkAssistant.cpp](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/BlinkAssistant.cpp); random look every 4000 ms to x,y ∈ [−0.5, 0.5] — [LookAssistant.cpp](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/LookAssistant.cpp).
- RoboEyes: default 50 fps (frameInterval 20 ms); autoblink every 1 s + random(0–3) s; idle re-position every 1 s + random(0–2) s; laugh (vertical shake) and confused (horizontal shake) one-shots last 500 ms; horizontal flicker amplitude 2 px, vertical 10 px — [RoboEyes header](https://github.com/FluxGarage/RoboEyes/blob/main/src/FluxGarage_RoboEyes.h).
- intellar: blink = 3 steps of −12 px height then back, corner radius remapped from 10 → 1 as the eye closes; wakeup = height grows 2 → 40 px in 2-px steps; saccade = 2 steps of 8 px (x) / 6 px (y) with the eye height squashed by 8 px during the move — [control_display.ino](https://github.com/intellar/oled_eye_display/blob/main/control_display/control_display.ino).
- pycozmo: 30 fps; expression transitions in its example take rate//3 = 10 frames (≈333 ms) of linear interpolation, then hold 1 s — [procedural_face_expressions.py](https://github.com/zayfod/pycozmo/blob/master/examples/procedural_face_expressions.py), [robot.py](https://github.com/zayfod/pycozmo/blob/master/pycozmo/robot.py).

**Human reference data**
- A typical blink is ~100 ms, long blinks up to ~400 ms (range attributed to Harvard BioNumbers; secondary source) — [Soma Technology blog](https://www.somatechnology.com/blog/thursday-thoughts/fast-average-blink); typical adult rate ~15–20 blinks/min, one magnetic-coil study measured 13.7 ± 7.8/min (search summary; primary studies listed at [Clinical Ophthalmology 2014 blink-subtype study](https://doaj.org/article/10199ccc2e884da298b3bfad2f69ca53) and [Harvard eye.hms entry](https://eye.hms.harvard.edu/node/1351177)). Treat as approximate.
- Saccades last tens of ms: ~31 ms for 5°, ~42 ms for 10°; duration ≈ 2.2 ms/deg + 21 ms; peak velocity rises with amplitude and saturates (~500 °/s model asymptote, up to 800–1000 °/s for very large saccades) — [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3491344/table/T3), [arXiv 2203.00488](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00488), [JEMR Duchowski](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/download/3603/Duchowski_JEMR_10-3-4/13042) (mapping of each number to each paper is from a search summary — verify before quoting precisely).

### Inferences
- **Frame budget:** at 30 fps (33 ms/frame — Vector's keyframe step) a whole blink is ~10 frames; at 50 fps (RoboEyes) ~16 frames. Human-like saccades (30–60 ms) are 1–2 frames: show a saccade as **1–2 in-between frames with a vertical squash (0.7–0.85)** then a 1-frame overshoot of 1 px and settle — this is the cartoon equivalent of motion blur that Vector uses.
- **Blink recipe for 1-bit (proposal, scaled from Vector):** for a 32-px-tall eye at 30 fps: 27 → 19 → 3 px tall (width +5 %, +20 %, +150 % capped to keep within screen) → closed 2-px line (width ~1.3–1.6x; the 5x stretch of Vector is too wide on 128 px unless eyes are small) → 5 → 22 → 29 → 32 px. Shrink corner radius proportionally (intellar/esp32-eyes do this) so the shape never degenerates. Variants: double blink (2 blinks separated by 100–200 ms, ~15–20 % of blinks), slow sleepy blink (close 200 ms, hold 300–600 ms, open 300 ms), one-eye "wink", "half blink" (to 50 %) as a micro-expression.
- **Breathing idle:** 1-px vertical bob of the whole face (or 1-px height change of the eyes) on a 3–4 s cycle with ease-in-out; on 1-bit this must be at least 1 px or it is invisible — use 1 px up for ~1.5 s, 1 px down for ~1.5 s (two discrete states), never sub-pixel.
- **Anticipation/overshoot recipes:** surprise = squash eyes to 85–90 % for 60–100 ms, then pop to 115–125 % (pycozmo's Surprise uses 1.25), settle to 110 % over 150 ms; happy bounce = face moves down 1–2 px (anticipation 1–2 frames) then up 3–4 px and back; looking = lead with the eyes, follow with "head" (whole-face offset lagging 1–2 frames, Vector's ShiftLagFraction hints at such lag).
- **Secondary action:** overlays (sweat drop, blush, Zzz, notes) animate on their own slower clocks (150–400 ms per frame) while eyes animate at 30 fps → layered motion reads as richer.
- **Exaggeration:** because pixels are coarse, exaggerate shapes 20–30 % beyond what looks right in a vector mock-up (pycozmo uses ±30° lid angles for anger — on 32-px eyes that is ~9 px of slope, clearly readable).

## 3. Emotion catalog (~40 states): shapes, lids, mouth, overlays, timing, and transitions

### Takeaway
Use a **small set of reusable primitives** (eye base shape, upper/lower lid masks with angle+bend, pupil/highlight, mouth glyphs, overlay sprites) and define each emotion as a preset + a short "performance" (entry anticipation, loop, exit). Tween between presets that share topology; **hide topology changes behind a blink or a 1–2-frame "pop"** (closed-eye line is the universal in-between because Vector/esp32-eyes blinks zero lids/radii).

### Cited Findings

**Transition rules (proposal):**
- *Same topology* (rounded rect/ring with lids → another lid configuration, size or position change): tween parameters with ease-in-out (smoothstep) over 150–250 ms for small changes and 300–500 ms for big mood shifts (pycozmo uses ≈333 ms, esp32-eyes 500 ms linear; RoboEyes' halving per frame gives a snappy ~60–80 ms ease-out at 50 fps). Interpolate angles as angles; clamp radii to ≤ min(w,h)/2 every frame (intellar's `calculate_safe_radius`).
- *Topology change* (ring → heart, ring → "> <", eyes → sunglasses, eyes → spirals): **cut on the closed frame of a blink** — the closed line is shape-agnostic because blinks zero out lids/radii (Vector, esp32-eyes). Alternative masks: a 1–2-frame "squash smear" (eye squashed to 20 % height and 120 % width), a pop (scale to 0 then overshoot new shape), or a quick look-away (shape swaps while the eyes are off-center/compressed).
- *Entry/exit structure:* every emotion = anticipation (1–3 f) → main pose with overshoot (2–5 f) → loop/hold (with keep-alive layer still active) → exit back to neutral via the same or a blink cut. Never hold a fully static frame > 2–3 s.
- *Overlays* (blush, Zzz, notes, sound waves) fade in/out with a 4-step Bayer dissolve (80 ms/step) or slide from the yellow band; they live on their own layer so the eye tween does not need to know about them.

## 4. Procedural/parametric approach: what fully describes an eye, interpolation, noise/jitter

### Takeaway
The most complete, proven parameterization is Anki's: per face 5 values (center x/y, scale x/y, angle) and per eye 19 geometric values (center, scale, angle, four corner radii in x and y, upper lid y/angle/bend, lower lid y/angle/bend) — plus color/glow params irrelevant for 1-bit. Simpler hobby libraries (esp32-eyes 12 params, RoboEyes ~6 params + mood flags) prove that ~10–12 params already cover 18–25 emotions on 128x64.

### Cited Findings
- **Cozmo (pycozmo) layout:** 43 floats = 5 face params (center_x, center_y, scale_x, scale_y, angle) + 19 per eye: center_x, center_y, scale_x, scale_y, angle, lower_inner_radius_x/y, lower_outer_radius_x/y, upper_inner_radius_x/y, upper_outer_radius_x/y (default 0.5), lid0 y/angle/bend, lid1 y/angle/bend (default 0). Lids are rectangles + a chord (bend) rotated by the lid angle and pasted in black over the eye; lid 1 is the lower lid (rotated 180°). Interpolation is linear per parameter over N steps — [pycozmo procedural_face.py](https://github.com/zayfod/pycozmo/blob/master/pycozmo/procedural_face.py); Cozmo .bin FlatBuffers animations describe faces procedurally with 19 coefficients per eye — [pycozmo issue (Lightrun mirror)](https://lightrun.com/answers/zayfod-pycozmo-procedural-face-rendering).
- **Vector parameter table with clip limits and combine rules:** EyeCenterX/Y (add; ±half screen), EyeScaleX/Y (multiply; 0–10), EyeAngle (add; ±360°), Lower/Upper Inner/Outer RadiusX/Y (0–1), UpperLidY (0–1), UpperLidAngle (add; ±45°), UpperLidBend (−1…1), LowerLidY, LowerLidAngle (±45°), LowerLidBend, plus Saturation, Lightness, GlowSize, HotSpotCenterX/Y, GlowLightness (color-only) — [proceduralFace.cpp kEyeParamInfoLUT](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFace.cpp). "Combine" lets layers (keep-alive darts, squints, blinks) add/multiply on top of the base face — same file, `Combine()` in [proceduralFace.h](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/cannedAnimLib/proceduralFace/proceduralFace.h).
- **esp32-eyes EyeConfig (12 params):** OffsetX, OffsetY, Height, Width, Slope_Top, Slope_Bottom, Radius_Top, Radius_Bottom, Inverse_Radius_Top, Inverse_Radius_Bottom, Inverse_Offset_Top, Inverse_Offset_Bottom; each emotion is a preset; transitions lerp all fields; a separate "transformation" (MoveX/Y, ScaleX/Y, 200 ms) handles gaze — [EyePresets.h](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/EyePresets.h), [EyeTransition.cpp](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/EyeTransition.cpp), [EyeTransformation.cpp](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/EyeTransformation.cpp). ### Inferences
- **Recommended parameter set for this keychain (proposal, 1-bit-friendly, ~16 per eye):** cx, cy, w, h, corner radius (top, bottom), upper lid {coverage 0–1, angle ±45°, bend −1…1}, lower lid {coverage, angle, bend}, pupil {dx, dy, r, visible}, highlight {dx, dy, r}, style enum {ring, filled} and ring stroke (2–4 px). Face-level: offset x/y (head motion), global scale, mouth {glyph id or parametric: width, openness, curvature, offset}, brow {y, angle} ×2, overlay flags. Store presets as int8/uint8 (fits in ~24 bytes per emotion → 40 emotions < 1 KB).
- **Layered composition (copy Vector):** final = base expression ⊕ gaze layer (add offsets, multiply scales) ⊕ blink layer (multiply h, w) ⊕ jitter layer (±1 px with low probability) ⊕ overlay sprites. Each layer has its own timer, so a "sad" face still blinks and darts.
- **Interpolation:** use smoothstep/ease-in-out instead of linear for preset changes; use critically damped springs or RoboEyes-style halving for gaze (feels responsive); quantize to integer pixels only at render time (keep floats/fixed-point internally to avoid 1-px "stair" jitter during slow tweens — or deliberately step 1 px at fixed intervals for slow drifts).
- **Jitter/noise for liveliness:** with 1-bit, lightness noise is impossible; replace with (a) 1-px random offset of one eye for 1 frame every 5–15 s, (b) ±1 px pupil tremor in "scared/pleading", (c) random variance of blink timing (uniform 3–10 s like Vector, with 15–20 % double blinks), (d) Perlin-like slow drift of gaze target (±2 px over 2–4 s).
- **Procedural vs sprite (artist's view):** procedural covers 70–80 % of states (all lid-based emotions, gaze, blinks, breathing, transitions) with tiny memory and infinite variation; sprites are better for iconographic, topology-changing frames (hearts, spirals, "> <", sunglasses, tears, Zzz, notes, glitches). Practical hybrid: procedural eyes + sprite overlays + a few full sprite "set-piece" animations (sneeze, dizzy, celebration, easter eggs).

## 5. Sprite/frame approach and asset pipeline (tools, converters, byte order, bicolor panels)

### Takeaway
Draw at 1:1 on a 128x64 (or per-element, e.g. 32x32) canvas with a strict 2-color indexed palette in Aseprite/Pixelorama/Piskel, use onion skinning, export PNG frames or a sprite sheet, then convert with image2cpp or a small Pillow script. The critical gotcha is **bit/byte layout**: SSD1306 RAM is vertical bytes per 8-row page (LSB = top), Adafruit `drawBitmap` expects horizontal rows MSB-first, XBM/`drawXBM` expects horizontal rows LSB-first.

### Cited Findings
**Drawing tools**
- Aseprite: color modes RGBA, **Indexed** (palettes up to 256 colors), Grayscale; animation with real-time preview and **onion skinning**; licensed under its own EULA (source available; some modules MIT) — [Aseprite README](https://github.com/aseprite/aseprite). Export: File → Export for numbered frame sequences (e.g. frame001.png), sprite sheets with horizontal/vertical/packed layouts and JSON metadata; CLI flags such as `--sheet`, `--frame-range`, `--ignore-empty` for batch pipelines — [Aseprite exporting docs](https://aseprite.org/docs/exporting/), [sprite sheet docs](https://aseprite.org/docs/sprite-sheet/), [CLI docs](https://www.aseprite.org/cli/), individual frames via Export not Save — [Aseprite community](https://community.aseprite.org/t/exporting-all-frames-individually/6846).
- Pixelorama (free, MIT, Godot-based, Windows/Linux/macOS/Web): timeline with layers/frames, onion skinning, frame tags, drawing during playback, "perfect pixel lines", indexed mode, exports PNG/APNG/spritesheets/GIF/video, and a **command-line export** for bulk automation — [Pixelorama README](https://github.com/Orama-Interactive/Pixelorama).
- Piskel: browser-based sprite/animation editor with offline desktop builds, Apache-2.0 — [Piskel README](https://github.com/piskelapp/piskel); onion skin toggle shown in tutorials — [Udemy lesson (via search)](https://www.udemy.com/tutorial/learn-to-create-pixel-art-for-your-game/piskel-image-editor/).

**Converters**
- image2cpp (javl, GPL-3.0, runs locally in the browser; built for a 128x64 mono OLED): draw modes **horizontal or vertical 1 bit per pixel**, 1-bit alpha map, RGB565, RGB888; outputs plain bytes, Arduino code (per image or combined), Adafruit GFXbitmapFont; dithering: binary threshold, Bayer, Floyd–Steinberg, Atkinson; scale/center/invert/rotate 90°/flip; batch import of multiple images; reading an existing byte array back to an image; bit-swapping, custom prefixes, ASCII-art preview — [image2cpp repo](https://github.com/javl/image2cpp).
- Pillow: converting to bilevel mode "1" uses **Floyd–Steinberg dithering by default**; with `dither=None` (Image.Dither.NONE) values > 127 become white (plain threshold) — [Pillow Image.py convert() docstring](https://github.com/python-pillow/Pillow/blob/main/src/PIL/Image.py).
- GIMP can export XBM that displays correctly with u8g2 `drawXBM`; an image2cpp export with little-endian horizontal scan also worked — [u8g2 discussion #2617](https://github.com/olikraus/u8g2/discussions/2617) (via proxy mirror in search).

**Byte layouts**
- Adafruit_GFX `drawBitmap`: row-major, each row padded to whole bytes (`byteWidth = (w+7)/8`), **MSB = leftmost pixel** (`b <<= 1`, test `0x80`); `drawXBitmap`: identical but bit order reversed, "left-to-right = LSB to MSB" (XBM) — [Adafruit_GFX.cpp](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-GFX-Library/blob/master/Adafruit_GFX.cpp).
- SSD1306 frame buffer (Adafruit driver): `buffer[x + (y/8)*WIDTH]`, bit `(y & 7)` → each byte is a **vertical strip of 8 pixels in one 8-row "page", LSB = top row**; a 128x64 frame = 8 pages x 128 bytes = 1024 bytes — [Adafruit_SSD1306.cpp](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit_SSD1306/blob/master/Adafruit_SSD1306.cpp).
- XBM data is packed horizontally with the first pixel in the least significant bit, the format u8g2 `drawXBMP()` expects — [teachmemicro](https://www.teachmemicro.com/?p=12417); u8g2 `drawXBM` draws solid by default, `setBitmapMode(1)` makes it transparent; `setDrawColor(2)` = XOR (not supported by drawCircle/drawDisc/drawEllipse); `drawBitmap` is deprecated in favor of drawXBM; full-buffer ("F") vs page-buffer (firstPage/nextPage, "always redraw everything") constructors — [u8g2 reference](https://github.com/olikraus/u8g2/wiki/u8g2reference).

### Inferences
- **Canvas setup (proposal):** two documents — (1) a full 128x64 "stage" with locked guide layers (eye centers, mouth band, the 16-row yellow line for bicolor panels, a 2-px safe margin) used for composing and for full-screen set pieces; (2) small element sheets (eyes 32x32, mouths 16x8, overlays 8x8–16x16, digits/icons) at 1:1. Palette: exactly two indexed colors (#000000 / #FFFFFF); turn off anti-aliasing and use pixel-perfect pencil. View at 400–800 % with a pixel grid and a 100 % preview window on a dark background.
- **Animate with onion skinning** ±1–2 frames; draw keys first (pose-to-pose), then breakdowns; check readability at 100 % and on hardware every few frames.
- **Element sizes vs memory (inference):** full frame 1024 B; a 32x32 element = 128 B; 32x16 = 64 B. 60 full frames = 60 KB, so prefer element sprites composited at runtime and delta-only animation; reserve full-frame sprites for rare set pieces. (Storage/compression design is covered by the firmware researcher.)
- **Which converter mode to pick:** Adafruit GFX `drawBitmap` → image2cpp "Horizontal – 1 bit per pixel" (MSB first); u8g2 `drawXBM` → XBM/LSB-first horizontal (or image2cpp with bit-swap); writing straight into the SSD1306 buffer/`memcpy` per page → "Vertical – 1 bit per pixel" (LSB = top). Keep sprite heights multiples of 8 and y positions page-aligned if you want to blit by memcpy without shifting.
- **Custom Pillow pipeline (proposal):** `Image.open(png).convert('L')` → threshold (`point(lambda v: 255 if v>127 else 0)`) → `convert('1', dither=Image.Dither.NONE)` → pack bits per target layout → emit `const uint8_t name[] PROGMEM = {...}` plus width/height/frame-count/durations from Aseprite's JSON. For GIF/video sources: extract frames with Pillow `ImageSequence` or ffmpeg (`-vf scale=128:64:flags=neighbor`), then threshold or ordered-dither (screen-anchored Bayer) — never Floyd–Steinberg for animation (Pillow's default).
- **Bicolor (yellow/blue) panels:** design overlays and status in rows 0–15, face in 16–63; do not animate a shape across the boundary (it changes color mid-shape); preview with the actual stripe colors.

### Gaps
- LCD Assistant (old Windows converter) settings and U8g2 `bdfconv` (for icon fonts) could not be verified because the search budget was exhausted.
- No permissively licensed, ready-made 1-bit robot-face asset pack was verified (LottieFiles/Dribbble/itch packs not checked).

## 6. Previewing and iterating (simulators, desktop previewers, OLED-look rendering)

### Takeaway
Iterate on desktop first: Wokwi simulates ESP32 + SSD1306 in the browser with Adafruit/u8g2 code, and a Python/PIL previewer (pycozmo already renders Cozmo-style parametric faces into 1-bit 128x64 PIL images) can export GIFs of every emotion for review; then validate on real hardware because pixel gaps, brightness and viewing distance change how strokes read.

### Cited Findings
- Wokwi: browser-based simulator (AVR, ARM, ESP32 incl. S2/S3/C3/C6, RISC-V) with SSD1306 as a built-in part; projects declare libraries (e.g. Adafruit GFX/SSD1306) in libraries.txt; Arduino-ESP32, MicroPython and ESP-IDF supported; VS Code integration — [mikrocontroller.net news](https://www.mikrocontroller.net/news/wokwi-schaltungs-und-softwaresimulation-im-browser), [Fab Academy guide (XIAO ESP32C3 + SSD1306)](https://fabacademy.org/2025/labs/chaihuo/students/chonkit-kuok/docs/week4/week4_microcontroller_simulation), [example Wokwi SSD1306 project](https://wokwi.com/projects/384777785643085825), [Wokwi VS Code docs mirror](https://www.mintlify.com/wokwi/wokwi-docs/vscode/getting-started).
- Wokwi already hosts Cozmo-style eye ports for SSD1306 (e.g. a MicroPython "esp32_animated_face" port) — [Wokwi project](https://wokwi.com/projects/456004847994708993).
- pycozmo's `ProceduralFace.render()` draws faces with PIL in 1-bit mode on a 128x64 canvas and offers `interpolate(from, to, steps)` — usable as an off-device previewer — [procedural_face.py](https://github.com/zayfod/pycozmo/blob/master/pycozmo/procedural_face.py).
- RoboEyes has ports for MicroPython and TFT displays (useful for big-screen previews) — [RoboEyes README](https://github.com/FluxGarage/RoboEyes).
- image2cpp can turn a byte array back into an image (debugging hand-edited arrays) — [image2cpp repo](https://github.com/javl/image2cpp).

### Inferences
- **Previewer design (proposal):** a single Python module that implements the *same* parameter→pixels renderer as the firmware (or the firmware compiled for desktop with an SDL/Pygame backend) so presets are authored once. Features: sliders for all eye params, timeline for keyframes, "play keep-alive layers" toggle, export of GIF/APNG per emotion and a contact sheet of all emotions, export of presets to a C header.
- **OLED-look rendering:** nearest-neighbor upscale ×8–10, draw each lit pixel as a square of 90 % size (0.154/0.17 mm fill) with dark gaps, lit color ~#7FD8FF (blue), #FFFFFF (white) or #FFD200 for the top 16 rows of bicolor panels, add a subtle bloom (Gaussian blur of the lit layer at 15–25 % opacity) to mimic emissive glow, and a "physical size" mode that renders at true 22x11 mm on screen (via monitor DPI) for readability checks at arm's length.
- **Review loop:** (1) contact sheet at 100 % on a phone held at keychain distance; (2) on-device A/B of 2-px vs 3-px strokes; (3) record the real panel with a phone camera at high shutter speed to check perceived flicker.

## 8. Making the character feel alive and not repetitive (randomized idles, weighting, easter eggs, time of day, personality)

### Takeaway
Liveliness comes from **independent, randomized micro-behaviors layered over the current mood** (Vector: blinks 3–10 s, darts 1–2.25 s, noise layer) plus a **weighted, memory-aware selector** for larger idle animations (esp32-eyes: roulette-wheel weights). Add slow mood variables, time-of-day schedules, rare easter eggs and per-device personality weights so two keychains never behave identically.

### Cited Findings
- Vector runs keep-alive activities (blink, eye dart, eye noise) each with its own randomized "next performance time" and removes them when a canned animation needs full control — [trackLayerComponent.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/animProcess/src/cozmoAnim/animation/trackLayerComponent.cpp); intervals: blink 3–10 s, darts 1–2.25 s, max 30 s blink spacing for screen protection — [faceLayerManager.cpp](https://github.com/kercre123/victor/blob/snowboy/animProcess/src/cozmoAnim/animation/trackLayerManagers/faceLayerManager.cpp).
- esp32-eyes: random behavior is on by default; the next emotion is chosen by roulette-wheel over weights; random look every 4 s; random blink every 3.5 s — [README](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes), [FaceBehavior.cpp](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/FaceBehavior.cpp), [LookAssistant.cpp](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/LookAssistant.cpp), [BlinkAssistant.cpp](https://github.com/playfultechnology/esp32-eyes/blob/main/BlinkAssistant.cpp).
- RoboEyes: auto-blinker and idle mode with base interval + random variation (both in whole seconds) — [RoboEyes README](https://github.com/FluxGarage/RoboEyes).
- Cozmo's personality was the product's core: Pixar/DreamWorks animators, an animation director from Pixar, inspirations R2-D2, Short Circuit, WALL-E — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/06/27/cozmo/), [Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/cozmo-ai-robot-brings-wall-e-to-life/).
- EMO's "Emotion Engine" claims asymmetric reactions (wink, pout when interrupted) and context (weather) — [Keyi Robot](https://keyirobot.com/en-us/blogs/buying-guide/inside-emo-robots-expressive-design-and-how-it-connects-with-you) (marketing-level).
- Robot readability improves when the robot shows forethought (anticipation) and reacts to outcomes — [Takayama et al. 2011](https://www.wendyju.com/publications/151r-takayama.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Three time scales (proposal):** (1) micro, 0.03–3 s: blinks, darts, 1-px jitters, breathing — always on, uniform random intervals with variance; (2) meso, 10–90 s: idle "performances" (look around, yawn, stretch = eyes squash/stretch, hum = notes, check-the-user = look at center + small smile, hiccup) picked by weighted random; (3) macro, minutes–hours: mood state (energy, happiness, boredom, sleepiness) that drifts and decays and biases the weights.
- **Selector rules:** weighted random with **no immediate repeats** (keep last 3–5 picks and multiply their weight by 0.1–0.3), cooldowns per animation (e.g. ≥5 min for big set pieces), random variants of each idle (2–4 versions, mirrored left/right for free), and randomized parameters (duration ±20 %, amplitude ±1–2 px, direction).
- **Rare easter eggs:** 0.5–2 % chance per idle slot or tied to dates/counters: sneeze, "game over x x" then revive, cyclops mode for a second, eyes swap sides, glitch "reboot", tiny dance, a heart on the user's birthday, special face on 100th day of ownership.
- **Time of day:** morning wake-up sequence on first interaction, more yawns after ~22:00, sleep at night (dim, slower breathing), sleepy-after-lunch dip; long inactivity → bored → sleepy → asleep chain (e.g. 2 min / 5 min / 10 min — tune).
- **Personality per device:** seed a few trait multipliers at first boot (playful, shy, grumpy, curious) that scale weights (e.g. shy → more look-away and blush), and let user interactions (petting/shaking/talking) slowly shift traits — gives "my pet is unique".
- **Reaction priority:** event reactions (tap, shake, voice, charging, low battery) interrupt idles with an anticipation frame, play, then return to mood baseline via a blink cut; after a reaction, suppress the same reaction's big version for 20–60 s and play a smaller variant to avoid "robotic" repetition.
- **Screen-protection angle:** keep-alive motion doubles as OLED burn-in mitigation (Vector explicitly caps blink spacing for "screen protection"); never hold identical lit pixels for long — even the sleeping face should drift 1–2 px over minutes.

### Gaps
- No primary source on how Dasai Mochi or EMO schedule idle animations or weight them.
- No user-research data found on how many distinct animations are needed before users perceive repetition; the 3–5-item no-repeat memory and cooldowns are heuristics.
